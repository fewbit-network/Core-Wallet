## Abstract

FewBit is a community-driven hybrid blockchain combining Proof-of-Work mining, SmartNode infrastructure, decentralized governance, and a reward model designed to prioritize the long-term operation of the network.

Built around the GhostRider mining algorithm and a SmartNode-based service architecture, FewBit aims to balance block production, network security, community participation, and sustainable development funding.

At block 600000, FewBit introduced a major change to its reward distribution, allocating 90% of block rewards to SmartNodes, 5% to miners, and 5% to the Development Fund. This change reflects a deliberate emphasis on the infrastructure supporting the blockchain while retaining incentives for mining and continued development.

The SharedNode initiative extends this model by providing a way for multiple participants to contribute collectively to SmartNode infrastructure and share rewards according to the applicable participation rules.

FewBit's long-term vision is to create an ecosystem in which mining, operating infrastructure, holding collateral, contributing to development, and participating in the community reinforce one another.

**Core principle: Mine the blocks. Operate the infrastructure. Build the future together.**

# 1. Introduction

## 1.1 The Challenge

Many blockchain ecosystems depend heavily on mining incentives, short-term speculation, or centralized infrastructure. These approaches can make it difficult to sustain a strong community of independent operators over time.

A blockchain requires more than block production. It also needs reliable infrastructure, distributed participation, secure network services, ongoing software development, and a funding model capable of supporting future improvements.

FewBit addresses these needs through a hybrid architecture combining Proof-of-Work and SmartNodes.

Instead of treating mining as the only meaningful form of network participation, FewBit gives SmartNode infrastructure a central role in its economic model.

## 1.2 The FewBit Approach

FewBit is built around five principles:

1. **Decentralization:** Encourage independent participation in network infrastructure.
2. **Sustainable incentives:** Direct the majority of block rewards toward SmartNodes.
3. **Accessible participation:** Develop shared infrastructure models that can broaden community involvement.
4. **Transparent development:** Allocate a defined share of block rewards to development.
5. **Long-term growth:** Evolve the protocol through open-source development and community participation.

These principles guide the project's economic design and its ongoing development.

## 1.3 No Premine

FewBit is designed around a no-premine distribution model.

This means the project does not rely on a predefined founder allocation created through a premine. New coins enter circulation through the blockchain's emission and reward mechanisms, subject to the protocol rules.

The project's historical reward structure and funding mechanisms are documented separately in this paper.

# 2. Blockchain Architecture

## 2.1 Hybrid Consensus and Infrastructure

FewBit combines two complementary components:

- **Proof-of-Work (PoW):** Miners compete to produce valid blocks.
- **SmartNodes:** Distributed infrastructure provides network services and participates in supported governance and quorum mechanisms.

Mining contributes to block production, while SmartNodes provide additional services that help the ecosystem operate.

This division of responsibilities is fundamental to FewBit's design.

## 2.2 GhostRider Proof-of-Work

FewBit uses GhostRider, adapted from Raptoreum, as its Proof-of-Work mining algorithm.

GhostRider was designed to support CPU and GPU mining through a sequence of hashing algorithms.

Mining serves several purposes:

- Producing new blocks.
- Processing and confirming transactions.
- Maintaining the Proof-of-Work component of blockchain security.
- Distributing newly issued coins according to the protocol's reward rules.

FewBit's target block interval is approximately 120 seconds.

Actual block intervals can vary depending on mining activity, network conditions, and difficulty adjustment.

## 2.3 SmartNode Infrastructure

SmartNodes are a core component of the FewBit ecosystem.

Eligible nodes provide network services and participate in the protocol mechanisms supported by the software. Their operation requires the appropriate collateral, configuration, and continued compliance with network requirements.

SmartNodes contribute to:

- Distributed network infrastructure.
- Quorum-based services.
- Supported blockchain security mechanisms.
- On-chain governance.
- Reward distribution under the active protocol rules.

SmartNode rewards are determined by the consensus and service mechanisms implemented by the network. Operating a node does not guarantee uninterrupted rewards, since eligibility and network conditions can change.

## 2.4 Quorums and Network Security

FewBit's technology stack incorporates Dash-derived mechanisms designed to support advanced network services.

These include:

- **Long-Lived Masternode Quorums (LLMQs):** Quorum structures used by supported network services.
- **Distributed Key Generation (DKG):** A process used to establish quorum cryptographic material.
- **ChainLocks:** A quorum-based mechanism intended to protect against certain blockchain reorganizations.
- **InstantSend/InstantLocks:** Transaction-locking mechanisms supported by the underlying technology.
- **CoinJoin:** Privacy-oriented transaction mixing functionality inherited from the technology stack.

These components serve different purposes and depend on correct protocol configuration, quorum availability, and software compatibility.

Their availability and operational status should be verified against the relevant FewBit release.

## 2.5 Open-Source Core

FewBit Core is distributed under the MIT License.

The source code allows contributors to inspect, build, test, and improve the software. Official releases provide reference versions for users and network operators.

Open development supports transparency, independent review, and community participation.

# 3. Economic Model and Tokenomics

## 3.1 FBIT as the Network Asset

FBIT is the native asset of the FewBit blockchain.

It is used within the ecosystem for transactions, SmartNode collateral, and participation in supported network and community services.

The economic model links several activities:

- Mining produces blocks and introduces new rewards.
- SmartNodes receive the dominant share of block rewards.
- The Development Fund receives a defined allocation.
- SharedNode participation is intended to broaden access to shared SmartNode infrastructure.

These mechanisms connect the distribution of FBIT to the operation and development of the network.

## 3.2 Block Reward Distribution

FewBit introduced a revised reward distribution at block 600000.

### Current allocation

| Recipient | Share of block rewards |
|---|---:|
| SmartNodes | 90% |
| Miners | 5% |
| Development Fund | 5% |
| **Total** | **100%** |

This replaced the previous allocation of 65% to SmartNodes, 30% to miners, and 5% to the Development Fund.

The change places a substantially greater share of rewards behind the infrastructure supporting the network.

### Why prioritize SmartNodes?

A blockchain ecosystem needs independent operators who maintain network services and support the availability of its infrastructure.

By allocating 90% of block rewards to SmartNodes, FewBit emphasizes their role in the network's long-term operation.

The model is intended to encourage participation in infrastructure rather than relying exclusively on mining rewards.

### Why retain mining rewards?

Miners remain essential to Proof-of-Work block production.

The 5% allocation preserves a direct mining reward while the network's economic model places greater emphasis on SmartNode infrastructure.

### Why fund development?

The Development Fund receives 5% of block rewards to support the continued evolution of the project.

Potential uses include software development, maintenance, infrastructure, testing, and other development activities consistent with the project's governance and funding arrangements.

A funding allocation does not by itself establish how funds are spent; transparent reporting and appropriate governance remain important.

## 3.3 Reward Transition at Block 600000

The block 600000 upgrade marked an important stage in FewBit's economic evolution.

The reward structure moved from:

- 65% SmartNodes
- 30% miners
- 5% Development Fund

to:

- 90% SmartNodes
- 5% miners
- 5% Development Fund

The change was intended to strengthen the relationship between network infrastructure and long-term participation.

Exact reward amounts, transaction outputs, and any applicable protocol fees are governed by the consensus rules implemented at the relevant block height.

## 3.4 Halving and Emission

FewBit uses reward reductions as part of its emission model.

At block 600000, the block reward was reduced from 25 FBIT to 12.5 FBIT.

Under the 12.5 FBIT reward schedule, the nominal allocation is:

| Recipient | Percentage | Nominal amount |
|---|---:|---:|
| SmartNodes | 90% | 11.25 FBIT |
| Miners | 5% | 0.625 FBIT |
| Development Fund | 5% | 0.625 FBIT |
| **Total** | **100%** | **12.5 FBIT** |

These amounts describe the nominal reward split before considering any additional transaction outputs or protocol-specific accounting details.

The halving reduces the nominal block subsidy and changes the rate at which new FBIT enters circulation.

Future emission schedules and activation heights must be interpreted according to the consensus rules implemented in FewBit Core.

## 3.5 SmartNode Collateral

SmartNodes require collateral to participate under the applicable protocol rules.

Following the block 600000 upgrade, the SmartNode collateral requirement became **112,500 FBIT**.

The next planned collateral update is associated with block 900000.

FewBit's collateral policy is designed to evolve alongside the network's reward and emission schedule. The exact requirements at each stage are determined by the applicable protocol version and activation rules.

Collateral is not the same as a network fee or a guaranteed return. It represents the amount required to satisfy the relevant SmartNode participation conditions.

## 3.6 Long-Term Incentives

FewBit's economic model is intended to connect participation with continued network operation.

The intended relationship is straightforward:

1. Miners produce blocks.
2. Block rewards distribute FBIT according to the protocol.
3. SmartNode operators receive the dominant share of rewards.
4. Operators can maintain their infrastructure and manage their collateral.
5. The Development Fund supports continued software development.

Actual outcomes depend on network activity, reward eligibility, operating expenses, market conditions, and future protocol changes.

FewBit does not guarantee profits or a fixed return to miners, SmartNode operators, or SharedNode participants.

# 4. SmartNodes and Long-Term Participation

## 4.1 Beyond Mining

FewBit views mining as one component of a broader network ecosystem.

Mining contributes to block production. SmartNodes contribute infrastructure and services.

Under the current reward allocation, SmartNodes receive 90% of nominal block rewards, making their role central to the economic design.

This creates an incentive for participants to consider not only the acquisition of FBIT but also the infrastructure required to operate the network.

## 4.2 Collateral and Reward Management

SmartNode operators must maintain the collateral and operational requirements applicable to their nodes.

Rewards can be retained, transferred, or used in accordance with the protocol and the operator's own decisions.

Some operators may choose to accumulate rewards toward additional collateral or future collateral requirements.

This possibility is a feature of the economic model, not a guaranteed outcome. The speed of accumulation depends on eligible rewards, node uptime, fees, collateral changes, and the operator's decisions.

## 4.3 Independent Infrastructure

FewBit encourages the participation of independent operators across different hosting environments.

Nodes may be operated on suitable self-managed hardware or through external hosting services, provided the setup satisfies the applicable technical requirements.

Geographically and operationally distributed infrastructure can improve resilience, although the actual degree of decentralization depends on the independence and distribution of operators.

The objective is to avoid unnecessary dependence on a single infrastructure provider or centralized point of failure.

# 5. SharedNode: Expanding Access

## 5.1 The Participation Challenge

Operating an individual SmartNode can require substantial collateral, technical knowledge, and ongoing infrastructure management.

These requirements can limit participation to users who have enough FBIT and the ability to operate a node.

FewBit's SharedNode initiative aims to create an additional participation model.

## 5.2 The SharedNode Concept

A SharedNode is intended to allow multiple participants to contribute FBIT collectively toward shared SmartNode infrastructure.

Instead of every participant operating a separate node, participants can take part through a shared arrangement.

Subject to the final implementation, the model can support:

- Contributions from multiple participants.
- Collective participation in SmartNode infrastructure.
- Distribution of rewards according to defined contribution and allocation rules.
- Broader community involvement in the FewBit ecosystem.

The purpose is to lower the operational barriers to participation while preserving the role of SmartNodes in the network.

## 5.3 Contribution and Reward Principles

A transparent SharedNode implementation should define:

1. How contributions are accepted and accounted for.
2. How participant balances and ownership records are maintained.
3. How rewards are calculated and distributed.
4. Which operating fees, if any, apply.
5. How withdrawals and changes in participation are handled.
6. How the service protects participant funds and records.
7. What happens during node downtime or protocol upgrades.

These are important operational requirements for any shared participation system.

The exact custody model, technical implementation, fees, and withdrawal conditions must be documented in the SharedNode service specification.

## 5.4 SharedNode and Decentralization

Shared participation can broaden access, but it is not automatically equivalent to independent node ownership.

A shared arrangement may introduce additional operational or custodial dependencies.

FewBit's objective is to make participation more accessible while maintaining transparency around how the service operates and how rewards are allocated.

SharedNode participation should not be interpreted as a guaranteed return or as a substitute for understanding the applicable service terms.

# 7. Security, Reliability, and Operational Risks

## 7.1 A Layered Security Model

FewBit combines Proof-of-Work mining with SmartNode-based services.

The two components address different aspects of network operation.

Proof-of-Work contributes to block production and chain security. SmartNode quorums support additional services, including applicable quorum-based protection mechanisms.

No single mechanism eliminates every possible security risk.

## 7.2 Protocol Upgrades

Consensus changes must be implemented consistently across participating nodes.

Operators should follow official release announcements, review upgrade instructions, and verify compatibility before upgrading production infrastructure.

Incompatible software versions, incomplete synchronization, misconfigured nodes, or unavailable quorum members can affect network services.

## 7.3 Wallet and Infrastructure Security

Users and operators should:

- Protect private keys and wallet backups.
- Download binaries only from trusted official sources.
- Verify release information before upgrading.
- Secure servers and restrict unnecessary network access.
- Monitor node synchronization and service status.
- Maintain reliable backups of important configuration and wallet data.

The security of the network depends not only on protocol design but also on correct operation by participants.

## 7.4 Economic and Participation Risks

FBIT is a cryptocurrency and may experience substantial price volatility.

Mining returns, SmartNode rewards, and SharedNode participation outcomes are not guaranteed.

Participants may face market risk, hosting costs, software failures, protocol changes, liquidity constraints, and other operational risks.

Shared participation services can introduce additional risks depending on their custody, accounting, and withdrawal arrangements.

This paper describes the project's design and objectives. It is not financial, investment, legal, or tax advice.

# 9. Decentralization and Sustainability

FewBit's approach to decentralization combines open-source software, Proof-of-Work mining, SmartNode infrastructure, and community participation.

Its reward model gives SmartNodes the largest share of block rewards, while the mining allocation supports block production and the Development Fund supports continued development.

The SharedNode initiative is intended to extend participation beyond users who can operate individual SmartNodes.

Together, these components express the project's central objective: a blockchain in which the people contributing to its infrastructure have a meaningful role in its ongoing operation.

Decentralization remains a continuous process rather than a feature that can be guaranteed by a single parameter.

The distribution of mining power, ownership of collateral, independence of node operators, concentration of service providers, and transparency of governance all influence the real-world outcome.

FewBit's long-term success depends on encouraging broad participation and continually improving the software and infrastructure supporting the network.

# 10. Conclusion

FewBit is built around a simple idea: a blockchain should reward not only the production of blocks, but also the infrastructure that supports its continued operation.

GhostRider mining provides Proof-of-Work block production. SmartNodes provide network services and receive the dominant share of block rewards. The Development Fund supports continued development, while the SharedNode initiative aims to broaden access to shared infrastructure.

The reward transition at block 600000 represents an important step in this direction.

With a community-driven development model, open-source software, and an emphasis on long-term participation, FewBit aims to build a sustainable blockchain ecosystem that can evolve alongside its community.
